## 2024-05-29 - [Python Datetime strptime Performance and Defaults in Loops]
**Learning:** Using `datetime.strptime()` inside a high-frequency loop (e.g., 30 FPS video processing) is not just a massive performance bottleneck (~100x slower than `time.time()`), it also introduces a latent bug. Parsing a time-only format like "%H:%M:%S" defaults the date to `1900-01-01`. When calculating `datetime.now() - parsed_time`, it results in thousands of days of difference. Relying on `timedelta.seconds` accidentally hides the bug because it wraps within 86400, but it remains dangerous and incredibly slow string parsing.
**Action:** Always use float timestamps (`time.time()`) and simple subtraction for internal application state tracking and rate-limiting. Keep string representations strictly at the edges (API responses and UI).
